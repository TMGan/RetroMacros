About Retro Macros

Retro Macros is the nutrition side of my coaching. You tell it about yourself, the foods you like and anything health-related, and it builds a 3-day food plan you can actually follow, with recipes and a grocery list. It's invite-only, for the people I coach.

How your plan is made

  1. Your calories

    Your daily burn is estimated from your height, weight, age and activity. For fat loss, your target is 20% below that, and never below what your body burns at rest or a safe minimum. To maintain, you eat about what you burn. To build muscle, about 10% more.

  2. Your protein, carbs and fat

    Protein is set from your body weight (or your goal weight, if you set one), fat never drops below 20% of your calories, and carbs fill the rest. You choose the style: balanced, higher protein, higher carb or lower carb.

  3. Your food

    Meals are built from the foods you picked, at the effort you chose, from "keep it simple" to "I like to cook". Everyday groceries, a vegetable at every main meal, and sauces and seasonings so the same foods don't taste the same every day.

  4. Checked before you see it

    Every plan is checked for your allergies, and each day's numbers are added up again. If anything is off, the plan is fixed before it reaches you.

  5. Adjusted as you go

    Your weekly weigh-in keeps the plan current. If fat loss stalls for two weeks, calories come down 100 a day. If you're losing faster than 1% of your body weight a week, they go up 100. Your numbers stay the same through the week, so nothing changes mid-plan.

Built around your health

  • Food first. Plans never suggest medications, weight-loss drugs or fat burners. The only extras are protein shakes (up to 2 a day) and creatine, which is recommended unless your health check says to ask your doctor first.
  • Health check. If you're pregnant or breastfeeding, have kidney disease, take medication for diabetes, or have a history of an eating disorder, your plan stays at maintenance calories until we talk.
  • GLP-1 medications. If you take one, like Ozempic or Zepbound, your plan switches to smaller, protein-first meals that are easier on your stomach.
  • Not medical advice. This is general nutrition guidance. Check with your doctor before changing your diet. Read the full disclaimer.

Who built it

Built by Tim Gangadeen, a personal trainer who also writes software. I built it because the same meal plan doesn't work for everyone. People stick with food they like, in portions that fit their life, so every plan here starts with the person.

How it's built

  • Java and Spring Boot on the server, with Thymeleaf pages.
  • PostgreSQL for accounts, plans, food logs and check-ins.
  • Google sign-in, invite-only, with a separate coach dashboard.
  • Claude AI writes the meals. The math stays in code: calorie targets, macro floors, allergy checks, daily totals and weekly adjustments are all calculated and verified by the app, not left to the AI.
  • Over 100 automated tests run before any change goes live.
